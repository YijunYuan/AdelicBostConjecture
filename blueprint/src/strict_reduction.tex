\chapter{Strictly stable maximizing counterexamples}

\begin{lemma}[The least counterexample rank sum]
\label{lem:minimal-counterexample-rank-sum}
\uses{lem:maximizing-semistable-pair,lem:normalized-semistable-extension,lem:degree-calculus}
\source{bost-specht-manuscript:page-0012}
\lean{BostConjecture.Arithmetic.Compactness.exists_minimal_positive_tensorMaxDegree,BostConjecture.Arithmetic.Compactness.isSemistable_tensor_of_smaller_rank_sum,BostConjecture.Arithmetic.HermitianMetric.isSemistable_iff_maxDegree_nonpos,BostConjecture.Arithmetic.HermitianMetric.maxDegree_tensor_pos_of_not_isSemistable}
\notready
If some positive-rank normalized semistable pair has nonsemistable
tensor product, there exist positive integers $a,b$ for which a tensor
maximum degree is positive, whereas every normalized semistable pair
of positive ranks $a',b'$ with $a'+b'<a+b$ has semistable tensor product.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A degree-zero metric is semistable exactly when all its induced
submodule degrees are nonpositive. Every induced degree is bounded
above by the maximum saturated degree, since saturation increases
degree. Conversely an actual saturated submodule attains that maximum.
Thus semistability is equivalent to nonpositivity of the maximum degree.
The tensor degree formula makes the tensor of normalized factors
degree zero. Its failure of semistability therefore gives a positive
maximum. Among sums of positive ranks for which such a positive value
is attained, choose the least natural number. Any positive maximum at
a smaller sum contradicts this choice. The same equivalence proves
semistability of every smaller normalized pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Minimal-rank strictly stable maximizing factors]
\label{lem:strict-maximizing-counterexample}
\uses{lem:line-twist,lem:minimal-counterexample-rank-sum,lem:normalized-semistable-extension,lem:semistable-normalization,lem:degree-calculus,lem:maximizing-semistable-pair}
\source{bost-specht-manuscript:page-0012,bost-specht-manuscript:page-0013}
\notready
Suppose that over a number field $K$ there is a positive-rank pair
of normalized semistable Hermitian projective $\Ocal_K$-modules
whose actual Hilbert tensor product is not semistable. Then there
are actual Hermitian projective modules $E,F$, of ranks $m,n\geq2$,
with strictly stable degree-zero metrics, and a saturated submodule
$S\subset E\otimes F$, such that
\[
 0<\degn(S)=\mathcal D(E\otimes F).
\]
Moreover $\mathcal D(E\otimes F)$ is the greatest value of the
tensor maximum degree among all actual normalized semistable pairs
of ranks $m,n$. This assertion imposes no freeness hypothesis.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Among positive-rank normalized semistable counterexample pairs choose
one whose rank sum $m+n$ is least. Such a least sum exists by
well-ordering of the natural numbers. The line-twist lemma shows
that neither rank can be one, so $m,n\geq2$. Every normalized
semistable pair of positive ranks with smaller sum has semistable
tensor product, by minimality.

The chosen tensor product has degree zero by the tensor degree formula.
Its failure of semistability therefore gives an integral submodule of
positive degree. Saturation preserves rank and increases degree,
so its maximum saturated degree is positive. The actual maximizing-pair
lemma now gives a normalized semistable pair $E,F$ of the same ranks
and a saturated submodule $S$ attaining its tensor maximum degree,
which is greatest among all such pairs. This maximum is at least
the positive value of the original pair, so $\degn(S)>0$.
In particular, this new pair is also a counterexample and still has
the minimal rank sum.

If $E$ had a nonzero proper saturated submodule $A$ of degree zero,
then $A$ is normalized semistable by restriction of the inequalities
for $E$. Its quotient $E/A$ is normalized semistable too: the inverse
image of a submodule of positive degree would, by degree additivity,
have positive degree in $E$. Both $A$ and $E/A$ are finite projective
of positive ranks strictly smaller than $m$: saturation gives a
torsion-free quotient, and over $\Ocal_K$ finite torsion-free modules
are projective. By minimality, both $A\otimes F$ and $(E/A)\otimes F$
are normalized semistable. Tensoring the exact sequence by the
projective module $F$ preserves exactness. Its induced and quotient
metrics agree with the Hilbert tensor metrics: in each archimedean
fiber the orthogonal decomposition of $E$ along $A$ tensors to the
orthogonal decomposition of $E\otimes F$ along $A\otimes F$.
The normalized extension lemma then makes $E\otimes F$ semistable,
a contradiction.

All proper nonzero saturated submodules of the semistable normalized
$E$ have nonpositive degree. The exclusion of equality therefore
makes all of these degrees strictly negative, which is strict
stability of $E$. The same argument, with the factors interchanged,
proves strict stability of $F$. These are the required factors and
attaining submodule.
\end{proof}
